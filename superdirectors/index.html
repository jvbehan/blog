Ireland's Superdirectors

Who sits on Ireland's boards?

Every blue dot in this cluster is one of a group of almost 2,500 Irish directors. Each line joins two directors who have sat on the same board. If this network looks densely connected, it's because it is.

Open records, close knit

Irish Collective Asset-management Vehicles (ICAVs) were introduced in 2015 as a purpose made legal entity type for investment funds.

Since then, over a thousand ICAVs have been set up, each with a board of directors.

Unlike other Irish corporates, ICAVs' filings are published by the Central Bank of Ireland. They include details of all current and past directorships of not only ICAVs but also all other companies...

...and they give us an insight into Ireland's network of Superdirectors.

The superdirectors

The 50 most central directors have held 23% of all board seats in the data.

They have sat on the boards of two-thirds of all ICAVs, as well as many other companies.

Two-thirds of the pairings among the 50 have been on the same board, though not always at the same time.

47 of the 50 are Irish.

A small world

Density is one way we can measure these firms' interconnectedness. To get the density of network, we count ICAV pairs sharing one or more directors as a percentage of the total possible ICAV pairings.

The higher the percentage, the more densely interconnected network, and the ICAV network stands out having held steady around 2.0% since 2022.

The last time statistics for large companies were published, Ireland was the most dense country measured worldwide at 1.5%.

For comparison, Finland (with a similar population and large company count) had a density of 0.3%.

Corporate directorship is a particularly small world here in Ireland, even more so among ICAVs.

Dear Chair

Around the dawn of ICAVs in 2015, the Central Bank of Ireland warned that "governance standards in the funds industry may be undermined as a result of a concentration at board level amongst a small number of directors."

In 2016, the ICAV network's density was very high at almost 4.7%. Much of this was due to the recent introduction of ICAVs: few had been formed and those that had were, even more than now, directed by industry specialists.

It's over ten years since the introduction of ICAVs and the Central Bank's letter. Density has fallen and the Central Bank says it has seen other governance improvements among fund manangement companies.

All that said, the concentration of directorships held by Ireland's Superdirectors remains very high by any outward-looking standard.
